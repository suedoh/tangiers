## Verdict: FAIL — all four primary cells

High checklist scores did **not** pick better trades. Setups scoring 3/4 or more lost **more**
than the same trade taken at any hour, in-sample and out-of-sample. 4/4 setups are too rare to
judge (~30 per side out-of-sample) and their intervals span zero.

## Why

1. **Before fees it is a coin flip.** Mean gross R is ≈0 for nearly every cell (score ≥3:
   −0.18 to +0.01). The checklist does not pick direction better than chance.
2. **Fees decide the sign.** "At a level" means a stop just past it — average risk 0.3% of
   price. Taker fee + slippage (0.16% round trip) then costs ≈0.5R per trade. The location and
   reward:risk boxes, which force those tight stops, are the two that hurt most alone
   (−0.5 to −0.65R); trend alone and flow alone sit at the baseline.
3. **Levels get poked through.** Win rate at levels is only 16–23%: price very often trades
   0.15% past a level before deciding — the same thing watched live at 74,909 on Nov 8–9.
4. **Score 4** shows positive gross R out-of-sample (+0.8 to +1.1) on ~30 trades a side, with
   in-sample negative. Too few to call; at most a lead to accumulate forward, not a signal.

## Deviations from the live checklist
Hand-picked levels (73,333, 74,909, …) excluded; VRVP recomputed daily instead of continuously.
Both pre-registered.

## For the learner (plain English)
- The 4 boxes are what traders look at, but on 6 years of BTC they did **not** find winning entries.
- Spots that scored 3/4 actually did **worse** than entering at a random hour, mostly because
  the stop sits so close that fees eat the trade, and price often pokes past a level first.
- 4/4 spots are rare (about 20 a year each way) and the result is too uncertain to trust.
- So: use the checklist to understand the market, not as odds. Waiting for a candle **close**
  beyond a level (instead of a stop right behind it) is the habit this result supports.
