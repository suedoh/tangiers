# Desk-trader entry watcher backtest - results (2026-11-10)

Pre-registration: [PREREG-2026-11-10.md](PREREG-2026-11-10.md) (written before any result).
Script: `scripts/research/desk-entry-test.js` · Raw numbers: [results-2026-11-10.json](results-2026-11-10.json)

## Verdict: FAIL - both primary cells

The setups lost money after fees in-sample, sat near zero out-of-sample on far too few trades,
and never beat random entries with the same stop and target distances.

| Cell (non-overlapping) | IS n | IS mean net R [95% CI] | OOS n | OOS mean net R [95% CI] | OOS gross R | OOS vs random twins |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| long, all setups | 128 | −0.78 [−1.21, −0.33] | 47 | −0.23 [−1.11, +0.89] | +0.59 | not different [−0.31, +1.77] |
| short, all setups | 143 | −0.67 [−1.08, −0.21] | 51 | −0.06 [−0.99, +1.24] | +0.64 | not different [−0.39, +1.91] |

IS = 2021-01-01 → 2024-12-31, OOS = 2025-01-01 → 2026-11-09. 39,789 level touches with a valid plan
(99.8% with OI); 190 long and 231 short alerts would have fired (≈6 a month in all). Random twins
(20 per trade, ±45 days, same stop/target %) averaged −0.6 to −0.8R. Neither cell clears BH or the
150-trade floor.

## Why
1. **Fees are bigger than the checklist's.** A stop 0.05% past the wick averages 0.3-0.4% of
   price, so the 0.16% round-trip cost is 0.6-0.8R per trade (checklist: ≈0.5R). The R:R ≥ 2
   rule does not protect against this: a full win nets +4 to +7R, but only 12-19% of trades win.
2. **The wick gets revisited.** 81-88% of trades hit the stop: price very often comes back
   through the rejection wick before moving away. Same lesson as the
   checklist's "levels get poked", one step later.
3. **Before fees, no stable edge.** Gross R ≈ 0 in-sample (−0.15 long, −0.03 short) and
   +0.6 out-of-sample on ~50 trades a side with wide intervals. The in-sample disagreement and
   the small count make the out-of-sample gross number a thing to watch forward, not a signal.
4. **The filters add nothing measurable.** Dropping the flow box or the wick box costs 0.0-0.4R
   out-of-sample and nothing in-sample, well inside the noise; dropping the trend bias makes it
   ~20 alerts a month at −0.7R in both periods. 1h-only was positive
   out-of-sample (9 and 13 trades) but negative in-sample: noise at that size.

## Checks
- Three trades (a stop, two targets; 2022, 2024, 2025) re-checked against raw Binance REST
  candles independently of the cache: trigger candle, wick %, last-5m delta, stop price,
  exit candle and net R all match.
- Deviations from live (pre-registered): hand-picked levels excluded, levels recomputed daily,
  1h OI measured over the full hour (the live watcher read only the last ~35 minutes; fixed
  in the watcher the same day).
- Found after the run, not pre-registered: the backtest reads the OI row stamped at the candle
  close, which live is often not yet published 20s after the close (live then reads the row 5
  minutes earlier). Dropping the whole flow box stays inside the noise and fails too, so this
  cannot move the verdict. Example: the 2026-11-10 03:00 UTC live alert read OI
  +0.01%; replayed with the later row it reads −0.00% and would not fire.

## For the learner (plain English)
- The alerts show what desk traders look for: trend, a level, a rejection candle, flow, a plan.
- On 6 years of BTC, those setups **lost money after fees**. A stop just past the wick is so
  close that fees eat most of a winning trade's edge, and price comes back to take out the
  wick about 85% of the time.
- Compared with the 4-box checklist (−0.6 to −0.8R per trade since 2025), these did less badly
  (−0.06 and −0.23R), but on only ~50 trades a side. That's too few to call it better.
- So: keep using the alerts to learn what a setup looks like, not as odds.
