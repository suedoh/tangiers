# Desk-trader entry watcher backtest - pre-registration (2026-11-10)

Written before any result was computed. The question: do the setups the live
"a desk trader would take this" watcher (`.claude/watchers/btc-entry-watch.js`) alerts on
make money after fees, and do they beat the same risk/reward taken at a random moment?
The user is a novice using these alerts as learning data; an honest null is a valid outcome.

Prior: the simpler 4-box checklist failed (notes/entry-checklist/RESULTS-2026-11-09.md):
a coin flip before fees, and stops just behind a level made fees ≈0.5R per trade. This
watcher answers the "levels get poked" finding by waiting for a rejection CLOSE and putting
the stop past the wick. Expected obstacle: its stops are even tighter (candle extreme +
0.05%), so fees cost a larger share of R; the R:R ≥ 2 after-fees rule is its only defense.

## Data
Same as the checklist test: Binance BTCUSDT perpetual 5m / 1h / 4h / 1d candles with
taker-buy volume (`.market-data-cache/ecl-*.json`, to 2026-11-09 21:10 UTC) and 5-min open
interest from data.binance.vision (`oi-archive-5m.json`, to 2026-11-08 23:55 UTC; OI gaps
read as unknown, never carried forward). 15m candles are built from three 5m candles
(skipped if any is missing).

## Evaluation points
Every 15m close and every 1h close, 2021-01-01 → data end.
In-sample: 2021-01-01 → 2024-12-31. Out-of-sample: 2025-01-01 → end. Split by entry time.

## The rules (identical to the live watcher except where noted)
At a trigger candle `c` (15m, and also the 1h at the top of the hour) closing at T:
1. **Bias** - 4h and daily EMA20/EMA50 stacks on closed bars (close > EMA20 > EMA50 = up).
   Both up ⇒ longs only, both down ⇒ shorts only, otherwise nothing.
2. **Levels** - previous UTC day high and low, 14-day VRVP VAL/POC/VAH (repo
   `buildVolumeProfile`, rowSize 34.7, recomputed once per UTC day), daily EMA200; merged
   within 0.15%. **Deviations:** the live watcher also uses hand-picked levels (73,333,
   74,909, ...), excluded here (hindsight); live recomputes the levels hourly.
3. **Trigger** - short: `c.high ≥ L·0.999` and `c.close < L`; long: `c.low ≤ L·1.001` and
   `c.close > L`. Rejection wick (short: high − max(open, close); long: min(open, close) −
   low) ≥ 40% of the candle's range.
4. **Flow** - the last 5m candle's delta (2·takerBuy − volume) in the trade's direction,
   and OI change over the trigger candle (open → close) ≥ 0. Unknown OI fails this box.
   **Deviation:** live reads only the last 8 OI rows, so for 1h triggers it measures the
   last ~35 minutes, not the whole hour; the intended full-candle window is tested here.
5. **Plan** - entry = the trigger close; stop = short high·1.0005 / long low·0.9995;
   target = the nearest level beyond entry by more than 0.1%; cost = 0.16% of entry;
   R:R after fees = (|target − entry| − cost) / (|entry − stop| + cost) ≥ 2.
6. **Cooldown** - 2 hours per (side, level) after a setup fires. 15m checked before 1h.

## The simulated trade
Resolved on 5m candles after T: first touch wins; stop and target in the same 5m candle =
the stop. Time stop 48h at that 5m close. Costs: taker 0.06% + slippage 0.02% per side,
charged in R. One position at a time per (side, cell), so trades never overlap. Entry at
the exact trigger close is optimistic (live alerts arrive ~20s later); costs cover part of it.

## Baseline: geometry-matched random entries
For each taken setup, 20 random 15m closes within ±45 days (seeded RNG), same side, same
stop distance in % and same target distance in %, same costs and exits. The setup's
baseline is the mean net R of its 20 twins; the test is on the paired difference. This
asks whether the setup picks better MOMENTS than chance with an identical payoff shape.

## Cells
Primary (2): long, all setups; short, all setups (15m and 1h pooled).
Exploratory only (not eligible to pass): 15m only, 1h only, no flow box, no wick box,
no bias box, and the random twins restricted to closes where the bias agrees.

## Pass bar - all of these, per primary cell
1. Out-of-sample mean net R > 0 with a day-clustered bootstrap 95% CI above 0 (10,000 resamples).
2. Out-of-sample paired difference vs the random twins > 0 with its bootstrap CI above 0.
3. In-sample mean net R > 0.
4. Survives Benjamini-Hochberg at q = 0.10 across the 2 primary cells (one-sided bootstrap
   p of mean R ≤ 0).
5. At least 150 out-of-sample trades (spec 07.1 floor). Positive but fewer ⇒ "too few to
   judge", never a pass.
Also reported, not gating: gross R, win rate (Wilson CI), average risk %, time-stop share,
lag-1 autocorrelation, setups per month.
